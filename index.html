Researcher at Humboldt-Universität zu Berlin and the Thünen Institute

Expected PhD: 2027

Urban & Spatial Economics · Labor Economics · Public Economics · Political Economy

In my dissertation, I study how place-based policies and public-sector activity shape local labor markets, spatial equilibrium, and political behavior.

Beyond my dissertation, I also study zoning and housing affordability and questions of tax incidence and pass-through. My methodological interests include causal inference, quantitative spatial models, and Bayesian machine learning.

Job Market Paper

The Effect of Public Sector Relocations on Regional Development in Germany

New draft available soon!

SOLE Poster Prize · 6th World Labor Conference, Toronto

Exploits a large Bavarian public-agency relocation program and linked administrative employer–employee data to estimate effects at both receiving and sending locations, then quantifies how public employment contributes to private agglomeration economies in a spatial-equilibrium model.

The Potential of Public Employment Reallocation as a Place-Based Policy

Forthcoming in The Economics of Place-Based Policies, ed. Cecile Gaubert, Gordon H. Hanson, and David Neumark, University of Chicago Press. NBER Working Paper 33432.

Synthesizes international evidence on capital relocations and public-agency decentralization to assess when moving government jobs can work as place-based policy—and whether the places losing them bear meaningful costs.

The Growth–Affordability Trade-off in Zoning

With Gabriel Ahlfeldt, Jan Bakker, and Nina Gläser

Combines matched employer–employee data with an original postcode-level zoning dataset covering all of Germany to discipline a spatial general-equilibrium model of how land-use regulation trades off housing affordability against agglomeration, productivity, welfare, and inequality.

Policy paper

35 cents less for gasoline and 17 cents less for diesel — The fuel tax reduction was passed on to consumers — on average

With S. Syga · ifo Dresden berichtet 29 (2022), 13–18

Uses Germany’s 2022 temporary fuel-tax cut and comparable European reforms to estimate pass-through at the pump, showing that tax relief reached consumers but with heterogeneous dynamics across countries and over time.

Working Papers

Working paper

Do place-based policies matter at the poll?

With Luisa Doerr and Florian Dorn

Exploits quasi-experimental variation from Bavaria’s rule-based public-agency relocations to estimate whether place-based economic policy changes voting behavior, providing evidence that targeted regional policy can translate into electoral responses.

PDF

Working paper

The Drivers of Far-Right Populism in Western Democracies

With Patrick Zwerschke

Rather than testing one favored explanation, compares more than 26 economic, cultural, and institutional drivers across more than 30 Western democracies using Bayesian Model Averaging and Bayesian Additive Regression Trees to identify robust and nonlinear relationships.

Working paper

A comment on Xu (2022) “Reshaping Global Trade: The Immediate and Long-Term Effects of Bank Failures”

With Guillaume Bérard and Priyam Verma

Reassesses the estimated long-run trade effects of the 1866 banking crisis through replication and modern robustness checks, identifying implementation issues while showing that the central result survives.
