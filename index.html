Job Market Paper

The Effect of Public Sector Relocations on Regional Development in Germany

I study a ten-year Bavarian policy that relocated 60 state agencies and around 3,000 mostly high-skilled public-sector jobs from metropolitan areas to lagging regions. Using administrative employer–employee data, I find that private employment rises in receiving regions without corresponding losses in the cities sending public jobs. I develop a quantitative spatial model with public- and private-sector agglomeration spillovers to explain this pattern and quantify how reallocating government employment reshapes productivity, spatial economic activity, and welfare.
