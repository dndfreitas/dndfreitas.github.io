Job Market Paper

The Effect of Public Sector Relocations on Regional Development in Germany

PDF · Please check for regular updates

Can governments create private sector jobs in lagging regions by relocating public sector employees? I study a program moving about 5,200 public sector jobs from thriving cities to lagging towns in one of Germany’s largest states. Using linked employer-employee social security records, I construct comparison groups outside the policy state by matching municipalities on the indicators that determine program eligibility. Within five years, private employment in receiving municipalities rises by 4.3%, with no offsetting decline in sending municipalities. Following individual public sector workers affected by the relocations, I find that jobs move, but people do not. A quantitative spatial model rationalizes these effects through public-to-private productivity spillovers at receiving locations and the release of floor space for private firms at sending locations. The relocations leave national output and welfare nearly unchanged.
