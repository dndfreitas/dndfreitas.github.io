Job Market Paper

The Effect of Public Sector Relocations on Regional Development in Germany

PDF · Please check for regular updates

Can governments create private jobs in lagging regions by relocating their own employees? I study a program in Germany’s second most populous state that is moving about 5,200 public-sector jobs from thriving cities to lagging towns. Using self-collected relocation data and linked employer–employee social security records, I compare treated municipalities with municipalities outside the policy state matched on the indicators that determine program eligibility. Within five years, private employment in municipalities that gain public jobs rises by 4.3% of its 2014 level, with no offsetting decline in those that lose them. Following individual workers, I find that jobs move, but people largely do not. A quantitative spatial model explains these effects through local public-to-private productivity spillovers and shows that the relocations leave national output and welfare nearly unchanged. Choosing destinations where public jobs raise private output most would cut the small output loss by about two thirds.
